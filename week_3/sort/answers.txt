sort1 uses: Bubble sort

How do you know?: Unlike sort3, sort1 was faster when using the sorted50000.txt file and slower with
reversed50000.txt. From this, I realized it was Bubble Sort, as its Big-Omega is Ω(n) and its Big-O is O(n²).
this indicates it performs faster on a list of sorted numbers than on one with reverse-sorted numbers.
However, it remains slower than Merge Sort.

sort2 uses: Merge sort

How do you know?: I discovered that even when changing the files, specifically reversed50000.txt and
sorted50000.txt, sort2 performed the sorting faster than the others. This is because, regardless of
how the numbers are shuffled, both Big-O and Big-Omega are (n log n), indicating that the best-case and
worst-case scenarios execute at the same speed.

sort3 uses: Selection sort

How do you know?: After analyzing the other two, I analyzed this one to be sure. I used the sorted50000.txt
and reversed50000.txt files, just as I had with the others, and observed that it took the same amount of
time for both files, given that they were the same size. From this, I realized it was Selection Sort, since
the worst-case and best-case scenarios, specifically Big-O and Big-Omega, are identical, both being O(n²).
